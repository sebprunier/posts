Cartes 1.0.0 is out: [sebprunier.github.io/cartes](https://sebprunier.github.io/cartes/).

{% include cover-image.html %}

I am both a developer and a town councillor (in Colombiers, in the Vienne department of France: 1,400 inhabitants). Cartes was born where those two worlds meet.

- As a councillor, we were missing a map of the town detailed enough to read every street, with our own data (collection points, risk areas…), and printable in large format for the town hall.
- As a developer, I knew everything already existed as open data. What was missing was a tool to put it together simply.

## What Cartes does

For any French municipality, Cartes produces a print-ready map, from A4 to A0:

- the map or aerial photographs from [IGN](https://www.ign.fr/), covering the exact territory of the municipality;
- public layers to overlay: land registry, local zoning plan (PLU), risks from [BRGM](https://www.brgm.fr/) ([Géorisques](https://www.georisques.gouv.fr/)), clay shrink-swell…;
- the municipality's own data, as GeoJSON, CSV, or from a simple list of addresses;
- the sources and the date of the data, written on the map as the licences require.

No account, nothing to install: a web page is all you need. A desktop application (Windows, macOS, Linux), a command line and an API round it off. Free, open source, and the municipality's files stay on your computer.

None of this would be possible without open public data: thanks to [IGN](https://www.ign.fr/) (the French national institute of geographic and forest information), [BRGM](https://www.brgm.fr/) and [data.gouv.fr](https://www.data.gouv.fr/) (DINUM).
